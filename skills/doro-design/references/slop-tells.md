# Directions to avoid

These looks appear regardless of topic. Do not pick them on an axis the user left open. If a direction shares one axis with them (a cream background, say), move that axis toward the product's own world.

1. Cream background, high-contrast serif headings, terracotta accent. Gyeonggi body is a serif, so this pull is strong.
2. Near-black background with one neon accent.
3. Newspaper or magazine layout: hairlines, zero radius, dense columns.
4. SaaS card set: equal rounded cards, one radius everywhere, the same soft gray shadow, gradient panels.
5. Template trim: small caps labels over every heading, `A · B · C` meta strings, tinted near-black text, monospace data labels, `→` on every link.

Also weak as directions:
- the industry default and its plain inversion
- the product name drawn literally (a "forest" app in forest green)
- a mock of the user's own tool (terminal, cockpit) on operate or read screens
- plain white, gray hairlines, small radius, no texture: blandness is a look too

Good sources come from the users' world: public signage, packaging and labels, places, notation systems, printed timetables and forms. Pick fresh ones for each product; do not reuse these examples. Spread the three directions over at least two kinds of source.

Ask of each mockup: would a similar product get the same thing? Is there one memorable element, and only one?
